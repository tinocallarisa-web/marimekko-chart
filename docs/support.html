A Marimekko (Mekko) chart for market structure: the width of each column is the size of the segment, the stack is who shares it. It answers "where are we strong in a big market?". The free version draws the full chart with three named series and Others. Pro adds up to 12 series, your company highlighted with a summary sentence, the change in share against a prior period, small multiples, horizontal bars, absolute values and labels with the value or the % of the total.

Quick Start

  1. Add Marimekko & Mekko Chart by TCViz to the page. With no fields it shows a start screen with these steps.
  2. Drag the segments of the market to Columns — channel, region, category.
  3. Drag who shares them to Series — company, brand, competitor — and the sales or volume to Value.
  4. The setup guide in the corner lists the optional fields and the style templates.
  5. Pro: type your company in Format → Focus series and add last year to Comparison value.

How to read it

Read it twice: across, which segments are big; up, who leads each one. A small share in a wide column is the biggest opportunity; a big share in a narrow column does not move the total much.

Selection and interaction

  • Click a cell to filter the rest of the page by its column and series; click a column label to select the whole column, or a legend item to select the whole series. Ctrl/Cmd + click adds; click again or click the background to clear.
  • Others carries every series inside it: selecting it filters by all of them.
  • Highlight: when another visual filters this one, the cells that are not highlighted are dimmed.
  • Bookmarks restore the selection. Right-click opens the Power BI context menu. A hierarchy in Columns gives drilldown.
  • When the report author turns interactions off, clicks do not filter.

Accessibility

  • Keyboard: Tab into the visual, arrows move between cells, Home/End jump to the first/last, Enter or Space selects, Esc clears, the context-menu key opens the menu. The tooltip opens on the focused cell.
  • Each cell has a description for screen readers with its column, series, value and share; the chart describes its columns and series.
  • High contrast: the visual uses the colours of the Windows contrast theme, with every cell outlined; the focus series is drawn in the selection colour.
  • The layout adapts to the size of the visual: labels are hidden when they do not fit, names are shortened, totals and axis are dropped in small panels, and the start screen has three layouts.

FAQ

How do I read a Marimekko chart?

The width of each column is the size of the segment; the height of each cell is its share inside that segment; so the area of a cell is its share of the whole market. Above each column you see its size and its % of the total. Wide columns are the segments that matter; a small share in a wide column is the biggest opportunity.

What is Others?

The series beyond the named ones, added up. They are never dropped: every column still adds up to its real 100%, so the widths do not change. Free names the 3 largest series; Pro up to 12. Click Others to select every series inside it.

How do I highlight my company?

Type its name in Format → Focus series (Pro), exactly as it appears in the data. The other series are dimmed and a sentence summarises its share and its strongest and weakest segments. If no series has that name, a note says so.

How do I show the change in share?

Add the same measure for last year (for example with SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR) to Comparison value (Pro). Each cell shows the change in its share in percentage points, and the summary sentence adds the total change and the segment that explains most of it.

Share of the column or % of the total?

Both are true. The share of the column answers who leads each segment; the % of the total answers how big that cell is in the whole market. The tooltip always shows both; the cell labels show one of them.

Why does a column look narrow?

Column widths are shares of what is in the chart, so they always add up to 100% of the filtered total. If a slicer or another visual filters the data, the widths follow that filter.

Can I use bars instead of columns, or absolute values?

Yes, in Format → Layout (Pro). Absolute values (bar Mekko) draw each column as tall as its size, so you compare both the size and the mix.

How many rows can it use?

Power BI sends up to 30,000 combinations of column, series and panel at a time. In the Power BI service the visual asks for further blocks, up to 20 more; in Power BI Desktop it uses the first block. A warning icon in the visual header says when rows were left out.

Do bookmarks keep my settings?

Bookmarks keep the cells you select and the filters of the page. Like every Power BI visual, they do not keep formatting.

What is the "Pro preview" watermark?

While you edit a report without a licence, Pro features you turn on are drawn working under a watermark that names them, and Power BI shows its licence notice. Reading view shows the free result, with a small note naming the Pro features it leaves out. Where Power BI provides no licence information (for example when you are not signed in to Power BI Desktop), the free result is shown.

How do I get Pro?

On the visual's Microsoft AppSource page, choose Buy now (not Install free). Licences are per user and managed by Microsoft; restart Power BI Desktop after the licence is assigned. See tcviz.com/licensing.

How are numbers formatted?

With the number format of the measure from the model, dynamic format strings included, in the report's language; sizes in totals and in the sentence are shortened (40.2 M).

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The visual makes no network requests and stores nothing outside your report. The links on the start screen open only when you click them, through Power BI.
